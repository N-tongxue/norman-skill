# Guided reading, parallel translation, and comparison

## Guided reading

Explain the problem and minimal background, then start with one consequential question. Ask one at a time and choose hints/follow-ups from the answer; if the user wants direct explanation, provide it. Move from factual understanding to mechanisms/assumptions and transfer. At the end, ask the reader to reconstruct question — design — evidence — boundary; stop when it is understood.

Record obstacles that actually affect understanding. Reuse terminology and Q IDs rather than repeating whole introductory explanations. Maintain a project learning log only when requested or already authorized; record sticking points and useful hints. One correct answer does not establish comprehensive mastery. Show the original image when teaching a figure/table.

## Full English–Chinese parallel reading

For a whole-paper request, establish a source map and pair Original / Chinese translation in paragraph/caption order. Preserve methods, numbers, symbols, qualifiers, and scientific citations. Keep translation separate from commentary and terminology consistent; do not translate "may" as confirmed. Mark questionable OCR blocks.

Place original figures/tables near their first substantive discussion, before their explanations. Preserve original equations or source crops. Keep a verifiable locator for each block. Complete long texts by section and record coverage; do not replace full parallel reading with an abstract summary. Avoid redistributing an entire source publicly without permission; respect the user's existing authorization and scope.

## Comparing specified papers

First establish necessary evidence for each paper, then compare question, assumptions, subjects/data, methods, metrics, results, limitations, and reproduction conditions under a common framework. Do not rank directly across different units, samples, or budgets.

Show agreements, conflicts, complementarities, and possible reasons, distinguishing established reasons from candidates. Citation relationships do not prove method adoption, intellectual inheritance, or agreement. Do not start large citation networks or bulk downloads without a task need. Preserve source/ID prefixes and original-image context for each paper.
